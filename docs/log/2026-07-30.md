## Oversized file split — sub-project F (`checker/widths/expr.rs` + `ops.rs`) closed

Scoped and executed in the same session: design spec
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-30-oversized-file-split-f-checker-widths-expr-ops-design.local.md`
and plan
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-30-oversized-file-split-f-checker-widths-expr-ops.local.md`,
all 3 tasks landed. Closes the out-of-scope finding sub-project E's own
`wc -l` sweep surfaced above — **not** part of R1 (R1 stayed fully
closed regardless of this plan's timing), a smaller, self-contained
follow-up.

**What moved:**

- `crates/mimz-core/src/checker/widths/expr.rs` (1,040 lines, flat file)
  → `expr/mod.rs` (835 — `check_expr`/`expect_ty`/`fit`/`check_cond`
  (top-down checking) + `infer_ty`/`ident_ty`/`field_ty` (bottom-up
  inference), kept together since `infer_ty` calls straight into
  `check_expr`/`fit`) + `expr/lvalue.rs` (213 — `lvalue_ty`/
  `index_in_range`/`mem_addr_in_range`/`slice_ty`/`const_bound`,
  assignment-target/index/slice typing).
- `crates/mimz-core/src/checker/widths/ops.rs` (1,011 lines, flat file)
  → `ops/mod.rs` (718 — the 4 free fns plus `unary_ty` through
  `replicate_ty`) + `ops/builtins.rs` (297 — `call_ty` alone: the four
  builtins, `min`/`max`/`abs`/`nand`/`nor`/`xnor`, and the
  `sync.double_flop`/`sync.pulse` argument-shape checks).
- Minimal split by design (one new sibling per cluster, not three or
  four) — a single extraction was sufficient to clear the 1,000-line
  threshold in both cases with comfortable margin.

**Purely mechanical**, plus one pre-flagged relative-path fix: `slice_ty`
(now in `expr/lvalue.rs`, one directory level deeper than the flat
`expr.rs` was) referenced its sibling via `super::ops::width_u32` —
correct when `expr.rs` was a direct child of `widths`, but `super` from
`lvalue.rs`'s own vantage now means `expr`, not `widths`. Fixed to
`super::super::ops::width_u32`, the exact same relative-path class
sub-project E hit twice with `super::names::Scope`.

**One real visibility finding beyond the design spec's own audit:**
`lvalue_ty`'s existing `pub(super)` did NOT carry over unchanged as the
spec assumed. The flat `expr.rs`'s `pub(super)` meant "visible in
`widths`" (its own parent); once `lvalue_ty` moved into
`expr/lvalue.rs` (one level deeper), `pub(super)` there means only
"visible in `widths::expr`" — too narrow for `widths::stmts`'s calls to
`lvalue_ty`. Caught immediately by `cargo build`, fixed with
`pub(in crate::checker::widths)` — the same pattern sub-project E's C1
used for `lookup_enum`. Applied the same reasoning proactively for
`call_ty` (Task 2) before the build even ran, since it's called from
`expr/mod.rs`'s `infer_ty`, a sibling of `ops` at the `widths` level —
avoided a predictable second round-trip. By contrast, `matched_ty`
needed **zero** bump despite also being a new cross-directory
relationship: it's called by `call_ty` from `ops/builtins.rs`, a
DESCENDANT of `ops`, and Rust's default (no `pub`) visibility already
covers descendant modules — only _sibling_ access needs `pub(super)`.
This session's practical rule of thumb: a plain `pub(super)` bump is
safe only when the caller stays a descendant of the moved item's new
parent; a caller that's a SIBLING of the new parent (not itself a
descendant) needs `pub(in crate::checker::widths)` instead.
